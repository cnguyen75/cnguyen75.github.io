side b. research

04

How receptivity models are evaluated

With Dr. Roberto Benzo and Dr. Peter Washington. Models that predict when someone will answer a health prompt are scored with a pooled metric. Across three cohorts of 63, 329 and 351 participants, 99.11 to 99.73% of the scored pairs compare different people, so the metric mostly rewards knowing who tends to respond. Within-person discrimination, the part an intervention needs, is much weaker. Backed by a pre-registered personalisation study.

05

Efficient training methods

With Prof. Eric Suess, CSU East Bay. A measurement study of five memory-saving techniques for fine-tuning small language models on a laptop. Together they raised the trainable step from 8,192 to 131,072 tokens in 71% of the baseline's memory. I also wrote a block-wise 8-bit AdamW from scratch that matched PyTorch's own to the byte and was statistically equivalent to full precision across six seeds.
phase 1 complete

Sleep validation study

The Lifestyles Lab at Ohio State, with Dr. Roberto Benzo. Co-leading the analysis plan for a study of whether a consumer Fitbit agrees with research-grade ActiGraph on total sleep time, wake after sleep onset, sleep efficiency and sleep latency in cancer survivors who wore both at once. The plan uses night-matched Bland–Altman analysis, mixed models and TOST equivalence testing.

side c. side projects

09

Pokémon TCG agent

An agent for Kaggle's Pokémon Trading Card Game AI competition, a hidden-information card game played on a live ranked ladder. A hand-written policy is wrapped in a whole-turn search over sampled hidden states, with a self-play-trained value network scoring positions. It finished 2,166th of 6,807 teams. Re-running identical local tests showed swings of 12 points, so most improvements smaller than that were noise.

10

Paper Trader

An automated ETF-trading system that wrote down its pass and fail criteria before any backtest ran. The first strategy was shelved when a ten-year backtest returned a Sharpe ratio of 0.74 against a required 1.06. Its successor runs on a paper account against a simulated twin: all 26 decisions since its clock started match, as of 12 October 2026, and it has already failed one of its own reliability criteria.
in progress

Inverse optimization of NBA shot decisions

Asks why three NBA players took the shots they did, using 128,069 shots from the 2014–15 season. A PyTorch cost network trained with KKT-condition penalties learns the hidden cost each player seems to minimise. Defender distance ranked as the top factor for all three.

Drug–target interaction network

A dashboard that turns DrugBank into a network of 7,393 drugs, 2,981 human targets and 18,038 interactions, and suggests new drug–target links with the evidence behind each. For a drug the network has never seen, it ranks real targets above untested ones at 0.85 ROC-AUC, against 0.69 for a structural-similarity baseline; about 0.76 is the fairer figure for a genuinely new compound. Rebuilt in 2026 after I found leakage in my own first version.
