01

Evaluation platform for LLM agents

An agent that handles a mortgage file can't be judged on one run. I built the platform that runs our agents through scripted scenarios against cloned, sandboxed loans, with a simulated borrower on the other side and separate graders for what came out, how each turn went and whether it matched the rubric, so a change is measured before it ships.
02

Making model outputs reliable enough to act on

A model's answer is only useful if you know when to trust it. I built an experiments platform for comparing extraction and judgement approaches: the same extraction run several times and agreed field by field, LLM-as-judge scoring calibrated against human-checked sets, and validation inside the agent's turn so a bad output fails closed instead of flowing on.
03

Underwriting and follow-up agents

Underwriting is mostly asking borrowers for the right document and checking it when it arrives. I built agents that do that on a multi-turn framework with durable workflows, under a supervising layer that reviews their decisions, a person who approves before anything takes effect, and a path for the borrower to override a request; then moved the legacy workflow onto them one request type at a time and retired it.
04

Co-borrower and joint applications

Two people on one mortgage doubles every step. I built the joint-application lifecycle end to end: inviting a co-borrower or standing in for one, joining at rate lock, continuing alone, removal, disclosures scoped to each applicant, and routing to review when what was entered doesn't match what was verified.
05

Mobile banking apps

The Android app was a partial shell next to a finished iOS app. I built it out in Kotlin and Jetpack Compose to match iOS screen for screen, put snapshot tests on both platforms as blocking merge checks, and parallelised the CI and release pipelines so a change on either app ships the same way.
06

Disclosure timing and compliance

A mortgage has disclosures that must go out on particular days, in a particular order, and again when terms change. I built the timing: delivery dates and business-day rules, the clocks that follow from them, re-disclosure when pricing, terms or applicants change, and a test safety net over the sequencing and the cascades.

two parts · efficient fine-tuning on one laptop

10

Train it in a quarter of the memory

sep to oct 2026part one of two · continues in 11 →With Prof. Eric Suess, CSU East Bay. How far one laptop can push fine-tuning of a small language model, and what each memory-saving lever costs: five levers, measured one at a time and then stacked, took the largest trainable step from 8,192 to 131,072 tokens in 71% of the baseline's memory, for a throughput cost of 17 to 22%, all of it from checkpointing. A block-wise 8-bit AdamW written by hand matched torchao's optimizer state to the byte and was equivalent to full precision across six seeds, by margins fixed before the runs.

Does the guarantee survive more options?

oct 2026 to presentpart two of two · ← follows 10With Prof. Eric Suess, CSU East Bay. A Learn-then-Test abstention gate promises that at most 5% of the answers it accepts are wrong; calibrated on 5 options and deployed on 150, it lets through 7.2% on a hosted decision model and 7.7% on a 135M-parameter model fine-tuned to match it. The exact fix, calibrating per option count, is underpowered at 500 items a bin, and rank-normalising the score makes it exchangeable by construction and still leaks.

Pokémon TCG agent

aug to sep 2026An agent for Kaggle's Pokémon Trading Card Game AI competition, a hidden-information card game played on a live ranked ladder. two parts · why players take the shots they take

17

Why did they take that shot?

jan to mar 2025part one of two · continues in 18 →Asks why LeBron James, Stephen Curry and James Harden took the shots they did, using 128,069 shots from the 2014–15 season. A small network picks the real shot from plausible alternatives about half the time on held-out shots, against under a third by chance. Auditing it a year later, I found the original optimality conditions never touched the learned cost, and a pre-registered attempt to recover each player's cost ended in a negative result: without court position, defender distance is a consequence of the shot, not an input to it.
 18

A foot of space is worth eight seconds

oct 2026part two of two · ← follows 17Public player tracking from 2015–16 puts the defender before the decision. A foot of space at the catch is worth about eight seconds of shot clock to the league, nearly nine on threes and when trailing, and the defender takes most of it back before the release. Whether shooters are right to want it, the data can't say: three instruments were too weak to value the shots they passed up.
19

Drug–target interaction network

aug 2025, rebuilt 2026A dashboard that turns DrugBank into a network of 7,393 drugs, 2,981 human targets and 18,038 interactions, and suggests new drug–target links with the evidence behind each. For a drug the network has never seen, it ranks real targets above untested ones at 0.85 ROC-AUC, against 0.69 for a structural-similarity baseline; about 0.76 is the fairer figure for a new compound. Rebuilt in 2026 after I found leakage in my own first version.

Which optimizer trains a speech model best?

2025, rebuilt 2026Five optimizers fine-tune a small text-to-speech model to a new voice, five seeds each. Four could not be told apart within the spread across seeds, L-BFGS was clearly worse, and the standard metrics scored 44% of clips that failed to stop as error-free.
