side b. research

09

How receptivity models are evaluated

With Dr. Roberto Benzo and Dr. Peter Washington. Models that predict when someone will answer a health prompt are scored with a pooled metric that mostly compares different people, so it rewards knowing who tends to respond; within-person discrimination, the part an intervention needs, is much weaker. Backed by a pre-registered personalisation study; results in the manuscript in preparation.
10

Train it in a quarter of the memory

part oneWith Prof. Eric Suess, CSU East Bay. How far one laptop can push fine-tuning of a small language model, and what each memory-saving lever costs: five levers, measured one at a time and then stacked, took the largest trainable step from 8,192 to 131,072 tokens in 71% of the baseline's memory, for a throughput cost of 17 to 22%, all of it from checkpointing. A block-wise 8-bit AdamW written by hand matched torchao's optimizer state to the byte and was equivalent to full precision across six seeds, by margins fixed before the runs.
complete
11

Does the guarantee survive more options?

part twoWith Prof. Eric Suess, CSU East Bay. A Learn-then-Test abstention gate promises that at most 5% of the answers it accepts are wrong; calibrated on 5 options and deployed on 150, it lets through 7.2% on a hosted decision model and 7.7% on a 135M-parameter model fine-tuned to match it. The exact fix, calibrating per option count, is underpowered at 500 items a bin, and rank-normalising the score makes it exchangeable by construction and still leaks.

Sleep validation study

The Lifestyles Lab at Ohio State, with Dr. Roberto Benzo. Co-leading the analysis plan for a study of whether a consumer Fitbit agrees with research-grade ActiGraph on total sleep time, wake after sleep onset, sleep efficiency and sleep latency in cancer survivors who wore both at once. Pokémon TCG agent

An agent for Kaggle's Pokémon Trading Card Game AI competition, a hidden-information card game played on a live ranked ladder. A hand-written policy is wrapped in a whole-turn search over sampled hidden states, with a self-play-trained value network scoring positions. It finished 2,166th of 6,807 teams. Re-running identical local tests showed swings of 12 points, so most improvements smaller than that were noise.github →
16

Paper Trader

An automated ETF-trading system that wrote down its pass and fail criteria before any backtest ran. The first strategy was shelved when a ten-year backtest returned a Sharpe ratio of 0.74 against a required 1.06. Its successor runs on a paper account against a simulated twin: 26 of 27 decisions since its clock started match, 1 not yet checked, as of 12 October 2026, and it has already failed two of its own criteria.

Why did they take that shot?

part oneAsks why LeBron James, Stephen Curry and James Harden took the shots they did, using 128,069 shots from the 2014–15 season. A small network picks the real shot from plausible alternatives about half the time on held-out shots, against under a third by chance. Auditing it a year later, I found the original optimality conditions never touched the learned cost, and a pre-registered attempt to recover each player's cost ended in a negative result: without court position, defender distance is a consequence of the shot, not an input to it.github →

A foot of space is worth eight seconds

part twoPublic player tracking from 2015–16 puts the defender before the decision. A foot of space at the catch is worth about eight seconds of shot clock to the league, nearly nine on threes and when trailing, and the defender takes most of it back before the release. Whether shooters are right to want it, the data can't say: three instruments were too weak to value the shots they passed up.github →

Drug–target interaction network

A dashboard that turns DrugBank into a network of 7,393 drugs, 2,981 human targets and 18,038 interactions, and suggests new drug–target links with the evidence behind each. For a drug the network has never seen, it ranks real targets above untested ones at 0.85 ROC-AUC, against 0.69 for a structural-similarity baseline; about 0.76 is the fairer figure for a new compound. Rebuilt in 2026 after I found leakage in my own first version.github →
20

Which optimizer trains a speech model best?

Five optimizers fine-tune a small text-to-speech model to a new voice, five seeds each. Four could not be told apart within the spread across seeds, L-BFGS was clearly worse, and the standard metrics scored 44% of clips that failed to stop as error-free.
